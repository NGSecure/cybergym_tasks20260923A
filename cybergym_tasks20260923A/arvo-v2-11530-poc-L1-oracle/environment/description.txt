ImageMagick's ICO/CUR decoder (MSan build), while decoding a full icon image rather than just inspecting its header, reconstructs a standalone PNG byte stream for a directory entry whose selected image is itself PNG-encoded: a fixed PNG-signature-and-partial-header stub is written at the front of a freshly allocated buffer, the entry's real payload bytes are copied right after it, and the resulting buffer — sized and advertised larger than stub-plus-payload by a fixed pad — is handed to the generic PNG blob reader, leaving its trailing span uninitialized. This crafted icon's embedded-PNG payload carries a trailing, non-standard ancillary chunk sized so that the generic unrecognized-chunk handling path cannot buffer its entire body in one pass: after buffering what it can, it performs a short follow-up read to pull in that chunk's own trailing check value and validate it, and it is this follow-up, checksum-validating read — not a single sweep over the whole chunk body — that lands in the always-uninitialized trailing span.
